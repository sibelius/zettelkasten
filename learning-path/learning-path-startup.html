Learning Path Startup

  • learning-path
  • startup
  • business

Updated Nov 20, 2026

Edit on GitHub

A startup is a company searching for a product that people want, and a way to grow it. Most die from building something nobody wants, not from bad code.

Learn it in this order: find the problem, prove it, sell it, then scale it. Talk to customers every week, at every stage.

0. Foundations

  • read Paul Graham’s essays, start with How to Start a Startup and Do Things that Don’t Scale
  • watch YC Startup School
  • read Zero to One, The Lean Startup and The Hard Thing About Hard Things
  • learn disruption and why incumbents ignore small markets
  • learn to handle uncertainty and risk

1. Find a problem

  • pick a problem you understand from the inside, ideally one you have
  • write down who has the problem, how they solve it today, and how much it costs them
  • interview 20 potential customers without pitching. Read The Mom Test
  • look for the gap: what people hack together with spreadsheets and WhatsApp
  • decide what to build next by pain, not by what is fun to code

2. Build the MVP

  • build the smallest MVP that solves the core workflow
  • ship in weeks, not months, use boring technology you already know
  • do things manually behind the scenes before you automate them
  • charge from day one, money is the only real validation
  • learn product development as a flow, small batches and fast feedback

3. Product market fit

  • measure retention: do users come back without you asking
  • ask “how would you feel if you could no longer use this product?”, aim for 40% very disappointed
  • talk to the users who love it, find out why, and build more of that
  • pivot fast when it is not working, but change one thing at a time

4. Sell and grow

  • founders sell first: do the first 100 sales yourself before hiring sales
  • write a sales playbook: who to call, what to say, what objections come up
  • pick one channel and master it: outbound, content, partnerships, or product-led
  • build growth loops, not funnels
  • build an audience by writing in public
  • learn pricing: charge for value, raise prices, and test it
  • track the numbers that matter: revenue, growth rate, gross margin, CAC, LTV, burn and runway

5. Company and money

  • open the company with a good accountant (contador): CNPJ, LTDA or S.A., tax regime (Simples Nacional, Lucro Presumido, Lucro Real)
  • split equity between founders with vesting and a cliff
  • learn the Marco Legal das Startups, and the investment instruments used in Brazil: mútuo conversível and SAFE-like contracts
  • learn how venture capital works, read Venture Deals
  • bootstrap as long as you can, raise money to accelerate something that already works
  • learn blitzscaling: when speed matters more than efficiency
  • understand your investing and personal runway before you quit your job

6. Hire and build culture

  • hire slowly, fire fast, avoid the wrong hiring strategy
  • hire people better than you, and give them ownership
  • write down the culture before it writes itself, see creating a culture from scratch
  • build a hiring pipeline, see the hiring page
  • run the company in writing: docs, async updates, decisions recorded

7. Operate and scale

  • automate operations with code, see StartupOps at Woovi
  • keep a product vision: vision, strategy and tactics
  • set goals for the quarter and review them every week
  • read High Output Management to learn how to manage managers
  • learn from the PayPal mafia: great teams spread and build more companies

8. Capstone

  • a company with paying customers, a monthly update to investors or advisors, and one metric that grows every month
  • get startup mentorship or paid consulting when you are stuck
  • write what you learned, and share it
